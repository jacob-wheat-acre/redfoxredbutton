Fitness Hacker

Privacy Policy · effective January 1, 2027

Fitness Hacker turns your workouts into swings in a game. It does this entirely on your iPhone. This policy says what the app reads, where it keeps it, and what it does not do.

What the app collects

Nothing. The app has no server, no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no third-party code. It sends no data anywhere, to the developer or to anyone else.

Apple Health

With your permission, the app reads from Apple Health:

  • your workouts: their type, time, distance, calories and average heart rate;
  • your daily step count.

It reads this only to turn your training into swings, to show your week and your month, and to count the milestones in the medal case. It never writes to Apple Health. Health data is not used for advertising, is not sold, and is not shared with anyone. It never leaves your iPhone.

You can turn the app's access off at any time in Settings, Apps, Health, Data Access & Devices, Fitness Hacker.

What is kept on your iPhone

The app keeps its own save on your iPhone: where you stand on the route, the workouts it has counted (including any you log by hand), your weekly plan and your settings. It stays there. Deleting the app deletes it.

Notifications

If you allow them, the app shows notifications when a workout arrives and on the days you planned to train. They are made on your iPhone; no server sends them.

Purchases

The one purchase, Full Route, is handled by Apple through the App Store. The app learns only whether it was bought. It never sees your payment details.

Children

The app collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new one will be posted here with its date.
